Slow Bar Profit Analysis

Enter what you bought, assign beans to subscription tiers, then decide how many of the remaining bags go to retail and pour over. Totals update as you type.

1. Bean cost

Refresh from your Google Sheet (a supplier invoice or the starter sheet), load a .xlsx or .csv, or type into the table. Cup price and grams per cup are not on supplier invoices, so enter them here once; they are kept when you refresh. Retail bags are assumed to be the same size you buy.

Paste a link to your Google Sheet and press Refresh. A sheet shared as “Anyone with the link can view” loads right away. For a private sheet, sign in with Google and the app reads it with your own access.
Sets the suggested cup price from bean cost. Type your own cup price, or a margin for one bean, in the table to override it.

2. Play with the mix

Work top to bottom: subscriptions take bags first, and whatever is left can go to retail or pour over.

2a. Subscriptions

Set each tier's price and subscriber count, then pick which bean goes in each bag. Every subscriber in a tier gets the same bean(s): Tiers 1 and 2 get one bag, Tier 3 gets two. Bean cost comes from the bag cost of the beans you pick. Enter the shipping cost per subscriber below; the total is worked out from your subscriber counts. If your Google Sheet has a Tier column, refreshing fills in the beans for you (Tier 3 gets the Tier 1 and Tier 2 beans). You can change any of them here.

2b. Retail

Reserve whole bags for retail. Leave boxes blank to use the grey auto values. Unsold reserved bags still count as cost.

2c. Pour over

Reserve bags for pour over. The table shows how many cups those bags make, how many you must sell to break even, and profit if you sell a different number of cups. The price per cup defaults to the cup price in section 1, or to the suggested price for your target margin if that is blank. Type a price here to try a different one.
